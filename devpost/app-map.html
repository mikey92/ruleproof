Ruleproof, mapped

A compact guide to how Ruleproof is put together: one real action followed through the code, where to start when you want to change something, and the practice from this build worth reusing.

Snapshot of commit 197a5d7 plus the contest-name fix that follows it (October 3, 2026). The code will drift from this map; the paths and function names are the stable anchors.

What it is

Ruleproof reads a contest's official rules and turns them into a checklist (what to submit, what to build, who can enter, what to watch out for) with the deadline shown in your own time zone. An AI does the reading, but every item must quote the rules, and plain code checks each quote against the rules before the item is shown as proven.

The pagesrc/. Takes the rules, shows the brief beside the rules text, keeps contests and ticks in localStorage.
The quote checkershared/verify.ts. Finds every quote in the rules text, ignoring only spacing, case, quote-mark and dash styles, and bullets.
Timeshared/time.ts, shared/ics.ts. Turns the stated date, time and zone into an exact moment, shown in any zone; writes the calendar file.
The serverworker/. A Cloudflare Worker that asks the AI to read the rules (cached in Workers KV) and fetches rules pages for the link option.

One action, end to end: click an item, see its sentence

Reference route (three stops). Open the files in an editor and search for the anchors in code.

1. Where a quote becomes proof: shared/verify.ts → locate

indexText builds a normalized copy of the rules plus a map back to the original positions. locate normalizes the quote the same way and looks for it: an exact match, or (with an ellipsis) every part in order and close together. Shorter than 15 characters never counts. The result is the start and end in the original text, which is what lets the pane highlight the real sentence.

2. Where the click lands: src/views/Brief.tsx → select, ItemRow, Proof

Brief runs checkAll over the items, dates and judging criteria once per contest and turns every found span into a mark keyed i3, d0 or j1. Items with no span go to the "Not found in the rules" group, where they cannot be ticked. A proven item's Proof button calls select(key); on a phone it also opens the rules as a sheet.

3. Where the sentence lights up: src/views/RulesPane.tsx → segments, the scroll effect

segments cuts the rules text at every quote boundary, so overlapping quotes each get their own <mark>. The effect on selected finds the mark whose data-first holds the key and scrolls it a third of the way down the pane (instantly when the pane has just opened, smoothly otherwise). Clicking a mark calls back into Brief, which scrolls the checklist to the item.

To change this, start here

  • What the AI is asked to find (sections, item cap, date rules): INSTRUCTIONS and SCHEMA in worker/reader.ts. Bump PROMPT_VERSION so old cached readings aren't reused.
  • What counts as the same words: FOLD, BULLETS and MIN_QUOTE in shared/verify.ts, with a test in tests/verify.test.ts for every change.
  • How dates read ("Mon, Oct 26 · 2:00 PM PDT", zone short names like KST): formatInZone and SHORT in shared/time.ts.
  • Calendar reminders: the alarm(...) lines in shared/ics.ts.
  • Colors and type: the custom properties at the top of src/styles.css.
  • Which AI answers: LLM_RELAY_URL/LLM_RELAY_KEY or OPENAI_API_KEY, and LLM_MODEL (worker/llm.ts).

A practice to reuse

Make the AI show its work, then check the work with plain code. The open question in the spec was whether a model would copy quotes exactly enough for strict matching. Rather than loosening the match up front, the build measured it: 83 of 83 quotes matched on three real rules pages, and 51 of 51 checklist items on two pages the reader had never seen. The strict rule held, so "not found" keeps meaning "the rules don't say this".

The same move works anywhere an AI summarizes a source someone will act on (a lease, a grant call, a policy): require a verbatim quote per claim, verify it mechanically, and show the unverified claims separately instead of hiding them. Evidence in this repo: shared/verify.ts, tests/verify.test.ts, and the measurements under Revisions in devpost/checklist.md.
